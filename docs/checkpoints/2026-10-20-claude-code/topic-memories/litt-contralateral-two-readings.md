Rat 69's contralateral hemisphere carries U251N material: 4.90 % human reads, the donor genotype at 56 of 59
identity probes, Y dosage 0.88, the line's copy-number profile, and a transcriptome that is not its own core
diluted. **Two explanations -- cells that migrated in life, and core tissue carried across on the blade at
dissection -- cannot be separated by this design and never will be** (same frozen coronal slice, same slide, same
session; the record does not say whether a fresh blade was used between pieces).

Greg, 2026-09-27, first framing: even if it is carry-over, that is the positive finding for a neurosurgical
audience. Greg, same day, second and binding: **"don't definitively say that the instrument contamination is what
I'm measuring unless it's definitely true."** It is not definitely true.

**How to apply.** State what does not depend on the choice: tumour material is measurable at a site remote from the
implant, by two platforms that do not share a failure mode, at a level routine histology of such a piece would miss.
Put every instrument sentence in the conditional ("if it was carried, then a single pass of an instrument moved
tumour...") or the interrogative ("whether an instrument deposits tumour where it goes next, and how much, is what
this kind of measurement can answer"). Never rank the two readings, never assert a rate, never say anything about
human resections. Keep the anchor as OTHER PEOPLE'S measurement: malignant cells recovered from gloves or
instruments in 14 of 47 canine cancer operations (30 %), 57 % with involved margins against 19 % with clear ones,
P = 0.016 (Orjefelt, J Small Anim Pract 2026;67:528-533); seeding case reports Steinmetz 2001, Perrin 1998,
Bouillot-Eimer 2005, Bekar 2010. The experiment that settles it is target T2c, a cutting-order control (29 pieces
with no positive bound the false-positive rate at 0.10).
