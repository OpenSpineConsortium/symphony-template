The owner's standing brief (2026-10-25): readers get a polished, abridged, themed paper; the textbook stays whole behind it, tied to its experiments' ledgers.

The owner said on 2026-10-25 that SchwingNet's textbook site is good
but "too verbose and exposes the underlying raw artefacts via links too
much", and asked that AI Scientist Pro, "and perhaps any future
sessions", hide the underlying artifacts and present "a polished,
edited, abridged version which follows a theme": the story of an idea,
its relation to prior art, its methods, its results against benchmarks,
its conclusion and where the work goes next, "packed to the brim with
reference", drawn in the discipline's conventional figures, and argued
"as definitive, absolute, and revelatory as possible", on "unbiased
experimental methods and data". The owner added that none of the
textbook's construction is to be disregarded: its artifacts are, or are
to be, tied to specific experiments with CLAIM-compliant ledgers, so a
result can be reproduced in one click once the research loop is
packaged and importable (SchwingNet #293 begins with the block
framework).

So a reader-facing paper embeds its figures, text and references and
links to no raw artifact; each figure carries its run's id instead.
Its definitive tone is earned: every number comes from a recorded run,
for publication one whose CLAIM 2024 and REFORMS report passes
SchwingNet's `tools/report.py check --complete`; every sentence about
prior work cites its paper by DOI; and a sentence the evidence does not
carry is cut, not hedged. The textbook and its artifacts are never
thinned to make a paper, and an artifact not yet tied to its experiment
is cited as text, never as a result. docs/research/ai-scientist-pro-plan.md
holds the design.
