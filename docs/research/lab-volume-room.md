## What was measured

- df on /rs/rs_grp_oschome reported 5,881,937 GB free, in the gate word fetch's budget line. That is the whole Ceph pool: the folder is CephFS, and df and statvfs report the pool behind it, not the folder's quota.
- The folder's quota is its attribute ceph.quota.max_bytes, 21,991 GB as the status word rounds it up (20 TiB, which spinesurg-ct-nnunet's notes call 20 TB). What counts against it is ceph.dir.rbytes. Both are read with getfattr, with no walk of the tree. At 11:20 UTC the data folder held 13,010 GB and the shared datasets 136 GB; at 12:48 UTC the data folder held 13,528 GB. Those are two folders of the volume, and they are all the status word read: the volume's own ceph.dir.rbytes, what counts against the quota, was not read that day before SchwingNet #161. So the quota less those two folders, about 8.8 TB at 11:20, is not the room, and the failures below say the room was smaller.
- So the budgets of SchwingNet's grid/run-platform-fetch.sh and grid/run-xray-chain.sh could refuse nothing, since each took its 5 TB margin off df's figure. ## What SchwingNet does now (#161)

The owner's rule of 2026-10-23: "just make sure any datasets that are selected for storage know exactly how much space they will require and block those which would cause overflow, all or nothing datasets". There is no floor.

- Each fetch job reads the exact bytes it will land from its source before it downloads a byte (grid/fetch_size.py). Its sources are the Hugging Face tree, a Zenodo record, an S3 listing and IDC's series sizes. The S3 listing counts every object under the prefix, since s5cmd syncs the whole prefix whatever --include says. acquisition_fetch.py unpacks archives into raw/, so a zip counts by its unpacked size, read from its central directory by HTTP range requests, and a .tar by its own size once its first block shows a plain ustar tar and no compressor, since tarfile.open decompresses whatever it is given. A compressed tar, or a server that answers no range, states no exact size and is refused. So are the gdown, Kaggle and Redivis routes, until SchwingNet #163 gives them a reader.
- A job starts only when that size fits in the quota's room after what the admitted fetches still running have yet to land. The decision is made under one lock (grid/size-rule.sh). Otherwise the job ends with exit 4, having landed nothing. acquisition_fetch.sh runs with FLOOR_TB=0, so the rule stops nothing part-way.
- The room is read with getfattr, or with the acq environment's Python on a node with no getfattr. Where neither runs nothing is read and the fetch is refused, and df answers only on a mount `stat -f` names as neither ceph nor fuse. A squeue that does not answer, a running fetch nothing sizes, or a landing whose bytes cannot be read refuses too.
- Every attribute is read up to its first NUL and taken only as a count of 1 to 18 digits with no leading zero, which bash's arithmetic holds whole with any sum of a few. A value that is not such a count is no reading and admits nothing, and the readout names the attribute and how long it read.
- A job Slurm requeues after a preemption keeps its admission: its restart count is above 0 and its own id holds meta/SIZE. Deciding again would count the part it had landed twice and could strand it.
- The X-ray chain is checked at submit by the sizes its list states, their sum rounded up to the whole GB, under the same lock, held until the chain's size is recorded.
- `bash ~/SchwingNet/grid/oschome-quota.sh` prints the lab volume in wsuquota's layout, with a file limit where the quota sets one, what is owed and the room for a new fetch. The status word opens with the same block, and the folder totals it lists under the block are not the volume's use. Sourced from ~/.bashrc, grid/wsuquota.bash makes WSU's own wsuquota print the block after its home-directory readout, whatever wsuquota is in that shell: an alias, a function or the command.
- A second session tried to break the rule before #161 was marked ready. Its eight findings, and what was fixed for each, are in #161's body. It found the arithmetic sound for a reading that is a count, and missed a reading that is not one, which the owner's own getfattr showed.

## What it does not cover

- The X-ray chain's sets are not sized one by one, and an entry its list leaves null counts as nothing (#163).
- Writes no admission counts can still stop a set part-way: the bank and catalog stages', the jobs' TMPDIR (/rs/rs_grp_oschome/go2432/tmp, on the volume), other users' writes to the group's quota, and a limit on the number of files (ceph.quota.max_files), which the readout shows and the rule does not count.
- A fetch sent before #161 runs the script Slurm copied when it was queued, with the 5 TB floor, until it ends or is cancelled and sent again.
- A sparse member, or a hard link tarfile copies, can unpack a plain tar past its own size.
- How far ceph.dir.rbytes lags what a fetch has landed has not been measured on the grid. CephFS propagates its recursive statistics lazily, at the landing and at the volume's root alike. Nor have the reads on a compute node, what `stat -f` names the mount there, or a requeued job's restart count.

- SchwingNet #170 (pull request #171, merged at a654c95) added a second reader, the volume's size by stat. The size is taken only where the data root proves the mount gives bytes: the data root lies inside the volume once links and `..` are resolved, on the volume's device, holds at least a gigabyte by its own attribute, and its size by stat lies between two readings of that attribute; the volume's size must then be no less than the data root's. - The status word at a654c95, at 17:15 UTC, printed "stat gives the volume's size as 3 and the data root's as 126 on a mount it calls ceph, where the data root's ceph.dir.rbytes is 14061320335387". - SchwingNet #172 (draft pull request #173, branch claude/172, CI green at e1f68f7) adds a third reader where both fail: the sum of what the volume's own entries hold. Each folder counts its ceph.dir.rbytes, read whole, past any NUL; each file or link counts its own size by stat without -L, as CephFS counts it. The listing runs in a subshell under `set +f`, since the hf-token route decides under `set -f` and would otherwise list nothing.
- A second session's adversarial review of #173 found that it can still read the use too low, so #173 stays a draft. The floor bounds the sum, not the folder that holds the data root, so that folder read short, by a fault or by rstat's lag, is covered by its siblings: with the tests' stand-ins, a fetch 150 GB past the true room was admitted. Before Linux 5.1, CephFS's getattr gave every inode outside a snapshot device 0 (fs/ceph/inode.c at 4.19), and RHEL's kernels may keep that, so a CephFS mount at one of the entries would pass the device check. And two refusals have the wrong words. The six findings and their fixes are in #173's body; the reviewer's attack tests, its 34 mutants and the author's 25 are in a comment on #173. Reading it takes a job on a compute node, which waits for the owner's word as every grid job does.
