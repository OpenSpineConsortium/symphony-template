Source: the thread "Paired CT and X-ray datasets", 2026-10-23. The measurements are the answers of the gate words fetch and status that day (through .github/workflows/grid-accounts.yml, kept on this repository's branch `grid` under logs/). They are read beside SchwingNet's grid/ as SchwingNet #161 leaves it (issue #160), spinesurg-ct-nnunet's tools/acquisition_fetch.py and slurm/acquisition_fetch.sh at bf1432f, and Wayne State's knowledge-base article 20203, "How to check disk quota on the Grid" (https://services.wayne.edu/TDClient/277/Portal/KB/ArticleDet?ID=20203). ## What was measured

- df on /rs/rs_grp_oschome reported 5,881,937 GB free, in the gate word fetch's budget line. That is the whole Ceph pool: the folder is CephFS, and df and statvfs report the pool behind it, not the folder's quota.
- The folder's quota is its attribute ceph.quota.max_bytes, 21,991 GB as the status word rounds it up (20 TiB, which spinesurg-ct-nnunet's notes call 20 TB). What counts against it is ceph.dir.rbytes. Both are read with getfattr, with no walk of the tree. At 11:20 UTC the data folder held 13,010 GB and the shared datasets 136 GB; at 12:48 UTC the data folder held 13,528 GB. Those are two folders of the volume, and they are all the status word read: the volume's own ceph.dir.rbytes, what counts against the quota, was not read that day before SchwingNet #161. So the quota less those two folders, about 8.8 TB at 11:20, is not the room, and the failures below say the room was smaller.
- So the budgets of SchwingNet's grid/run-platform-fetch.sh and grid/run-xray-chain.sh could refuse nothing, since each took its 5 TB margin off df's figure. The only limit that could stop a fetch was acquisition_fetch.py's own. It reads the quota's free space from WSU's df-ceph, falling back to statvfs, and its default floor of 5 TB (`--floor-tb`, which acquisition_fetch.sh sets from FLOOR_TB) stops an acq or idc fetch part-way. The hf-token route's reacquire.py hf-get had no floor at all.
- The full NLST's line stated 2,305 GB, taken from its card. idc-index 25.0.5 gives 11,139,589 MB for its 203,111 CT series of 26,254 people (series_size_MB, in units of 10^6 bytes). The running fetch was at 2,926 GB, already past the stated size, and growing about 390 GB an hour.
- By 12:48 UTC three acquisition_fetch.py jobs had ended with exit 1, within two minutes of each other: AbdomenAtlas 2.0 Mini at 12:22 UTC (08:22 by the grid's clock, which keeps EDT), after six hours and 331 GB; LIDC-IDRI at 12:24, a minute after it started on the same node; and the full NLST at about 12:24, after eight and a half hours, at 3,169 GB. ReXGradient-160K's fetch, which has no floor, ended COMPLETED at about 12:22 with 156 GB. The status word does not say why the three ended. It shows the logs of the six oldest failed jobs and cuts the rest, NLST's among them, and its line shapes withhold the fetcher's STOP and FETCH_STATUS lines; the two logs it showed held no traceback in their last 400 lines.
- In acquisition_fetch.py at bf1432f an exit of 1 with no traceback is a SystemExit, and two raise one: s5cmd missing, which stops an S3 fetch before it starts, and the floor's STOP, raised when df-ceph's free figure falls under 5. df-ceph's TB is a TiB, since it prints the 20 TiB quota as 20 TB, so the floor is about 5.5 TB. Three ends together, one of them a fetch that stopped a minute after it started, fit the floor. If it was the floor, df-ceph read less than about 5.5 TB free at 12:22 UTC, while the data and shared folders held about 13.6 TB of the 21.99 TB quota, so the volume held about 3 TB or more besides them. That is inferred, not read: the readout SchwingNet #161 adds is the first reading of the volume's own use.
- At about 14:00 UTC the owner read the volume's attributes with getfattr in his own shell. ceph.quota.max_bytes printed as 21990232555520. ceph.dir.rbytes printed as 48 digits, 165744388008563119603013828275240916436124852417, and ceph.dir.rfiles as 25, 2363642212733507223600077. No count of bytes under a 20 TiB quota has 48 digits, so what printed is not the attribute's count as written. Bash's command substitution drops a NUL from what a command prints and joins whatever follows to the digits before it; whether such a NUL sits after the true value is not yet read (the owner was asked for `od -c` of the raw value and for `df-ceph oschome`). If the first 14 digits are the count, the volume used 16,574 GB and 5,416 GB (4.93 TiB) was free, under df-ceph's floor of 5, which would fit the three ends above. That is a guess until the paste comes.
- Bash's 64-bit arithmetic wraps a number that long without a word. SchwingNet's rule as #161 first stood took a value's leading digits and would have read the use as -2,467,321,568,302,047,039 and the room as 2.5 EB, so it would have admitted every fetch. The volume's subfolders, the landings and the data folder, read as plain counts.
- wsuquota is the one command the knowledge-base article gives. The article says it shows used, available and total, without saying for which area, and it names nothing for group storage. In the owner's run it printed the home directory alone ("Home Directory Disk Usage", a 4,000 GB total). WSU also installs /usr/local/scripts/wsu/df-ceph, which the article does not name: `df-ceph oschome` prints the group quota, its use and the share used, in TB, and acquisition_fetch.py reads its floor from it. It knows nothing of what the running fetches still have to land.

## What SchwingNet does now (#161)

The owner's rule of 2026-10-23: "just make sure any datasets that are selected for storage know exactly how much space they will require and block those which would cause overflow, all or nothing datasets". There is no floor.

- Each fetch job reads the exact bytes it will land from its source before it downloads a byte (grid/fetch_size.py). Its sources are the Hugging Face tree, a Zenodo record, an S3 listing and IDC's series sizes. The S3 listing counts every object under the prefix, since s5cmd syncs the whole prefix whatever --include says. acquisition_fetch.py unpacks archives into raw/, so a zip counts by its unpacked size, read from its central directory by HTTP range requests, and a .tar by its own size once its first block shows a plain ustar tar and no compressor, since tarfile.open decompresses whatever it is given. A compressed tar, or a server that answers no range, states no exact size and is refused. So are the gdown, Kaggle and Redivis routes, until SchwingNet #163 gives them a reader.
- A job starts only when that size fits in the quota's room after what the admitted fetches still running have yet to land. The decision is made under one lock (grid/size-rule.sh). Otherwise the job ends with exit 4, having landed nothing. acquisition_fetch.sh runs with FLOOR_TB=0, so the rule stops nothing part-way.
- The room is read with getfattr, or with the acq environment's Python on a node with no getfattr. Where neither runs nothing is read and the fetch is refused, and df answers only on a mount `stat -f` names as neither ceph nor fuse. A squeue that does not answer, a running fetch nothing sizes, or a landing whose bytes cannot be read refuses too.
- Every attribute is read up to its first NUL and taken only as a count of 1 to 18 digits with no leading zero, which bash's arithmetic holds whole with any sum of a few. A value that is not such a count is no reading and admits nothing, and the readout names the attribute and how long it read.
- A job Slurm requeues after a preemption keeps its admission: its restart count is above 0 and its own id holds meta/SIZE. Deciding again would count the part it had landed twice and could strand it.
- The X-ray chain is checked at submit by the sizes its list states, their sum rounded up to the whole GB, under the same lock, held until the chain's size is recorded.
- `bash ~/SchwingNet/grid/oschome-quota.sh` prints the lab volume in wsuquota's layout, with a file limit where the quota sets one, what is owed and the room for a new fetch. The status word opens with the same block, and the folder totals it lists under the block are not the volume's use. Sourced from ~/.bashrc, grid/wsuquota.bash makes WSU's own wsuquota print the block after its home-directory readout, whatever wsuquota is in that shell: an alias, a function or the command.
- A second session tried to break the rule before #161 was marked ready. Its eight findings, and what was fixed for each, are in #161's body. It found the arithmetic sound for a reading that is a count, and missed a reading that is not one, which the owner's own getfattr showed.

## What it does not cover

- The X-ray chain's sets are not sized one by one, and an entry its list leaves null counts as nothing (#163).
- Writes no admission counts can still stop a set part-way: the bank and catalog stages', the jobs' TMPDIR (/rs/rs_grp_oschome/go2432/tmp, on the volume), other users' writes to the group's quota, and a limit on the number of files (ceph.quota.max_files), which the readout shows and the rule does not count.
- A fetch sent before #161 runs the script Slurm copied when it was queued, with the 5 TB floor, until it ends or is cancelled and sent again.
- A sparse member, or a hard link tarfile copies, can unpack a plain tar past its own size.
- How far ceph.dir.rbytes lags what a fetch has landed has not been measured on the grid. CephFS propagates its recursive statistics lazily, at the landing and at the volume's root alike. Nor have the reads on a compute node, what `stat -f` names the mount there, or a requeued job's restart count.

## After #161: the volume's own use, read three ways

- At 15:39 UTC the first status word at SchwingNet's main after #161 (83354d0) read the quota whole, 21990232555520 bytes, and the volume's own ceph.dir.rbytes as no count: "48 characters up to its first NUL where a count has at most 18 digits". The status words of 16:26 and 16:57 UTC read the same. At 17:15 UTC the readout gave getfattr's whole value as 48 bytes, which the gate's output filter printed as [hidden]. So no NUL follows the digits, which the section above left open: getfattr itself returns the 48 digits the owner's shell printed, on the login node, while every folder under the volume reads as a plain count. Why the volume's own attribute reads so is not known; no count of bytes under this quota has more than 14 digits. From then on the size rule admitted no fetch, as all or nothing asks of a room it cannot read.
- SchwingNet #170 (pull request #171, merged at a654c95) added a second reader, the volume's size by stat. CephFS gives a folder's recursive bytes as its size where the mount says so (the kernel client's rbytes option, ceph-fuse's client_dirsize_rbytes), a path that does not pass through getxattr. The size is taken only where the data root proves the mount gives bytes: the data root lies inside the volume once links and `..` are resolved, on the volume's device, holds at least a gigabyte by its own attribute, and its size by stat lies between two readings of that attribute; the volume's size must then be no less than the data root's. A second session's review of it is in #171's body.
- The status word at a654c95, at 17:15 UTC, printed "stat gives the volume's size as 3 and the data root's as 126 on a mount it calls ceph, where the data root's ceph.dir.rbytes is 14061320335387". The grid's kernel mount gives a folder's entry count as its size, the client's default (norbytes), so the proof failed as it was built to, and fetches stayed refused. The volume's own folder holds three entries, and the data root held 14,061 GB.
- SchwingNet #172 (draft pull request #173, branch claude/172, CI green at e1f68f7) adds a third reader where both fail: the sum of what the volume's own entries hold. Each folder counts its ceph.dir.rbytes, read whole, past any NUL; each file or link counts its own size by stat without -L, as CephFS counts it. The sum counts only where every entry lies on the volume's device and reads as a count, the entries listed are as many as stat gives as the volume's size, which on this mount is its entry count, and the sum is no less than the data root's attribute. The listing runs in a subshell under `set +f`, since the hf-token route decides under `set -f` and would otherwise list nothing.
- A second session's adversarial review of #173 found that it can still read the use too low, so #173 stays a draft. The floor bounds the sum, not the folder that holds the data root, so that folder read short, by a fault or by rstat's lag, is covered by its siblings: with the tests' stand-ins, a fetch 150 GB past the true room was admitted. Digits followed only by NULs, the shape kernels 4.19 to 5.1 give when getxattr's buffer is exactly the value's length, are taken whole, though neither getfattr nor Python reads with such a buffer. Before Linux 5.1, CephFS's getattr gave every inode outside a snapshot device 0 (fs/ceph/inode.c at 4.19), and RHEL's kernels may keep that, so a CephFS mount at one of the entries would pass the device check. And two refusals have the wrong words. The six findings and their fixes are in #173's body; the reviewer's attack tests, its 34 mutants and the author's 25 are in a comment on #173. The reviewer's six-line sketch of the first fix passed the 33 tests of QuotaReadoutTest and SizeRuleTest and refused that attack, in a scratch copy at 18:17 UTC.
- One limit no reading of the entries removes: a folder whose attribute reads a plain count below what it holds passes every check, since only the folder that holds the data root is bounded from below. Only the volume's own attribute read as a count, or WSU's `df-ceph oschome`, would show it.
