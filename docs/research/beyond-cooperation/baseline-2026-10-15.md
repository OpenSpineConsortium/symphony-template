How to read it, as a baseline of this project's own way of working:

- What the session is. A thread of a claude.ai Project in the cloud, started by a brief and two messages delivered into it since (the 3 prompts), with one helper, the review agent, whose calls are counted and whose context is not; the harness added 205 notes of its own (hook output and reminders), which the growth attribution cannot tell from the tools' answers they came with. The session's conversation was condensed once (the compaction), which is the drop from a peak of 379,356 tokens to the later calls' 184,764.
- The start. The first API call carried 120,867 tokens before any work: 43,468 read from a cache shared with sessions of its kind, the harness's prompt and its tools' schemas, and 77,397 written for this session, the project's instructions (about 18,000 tokens) and the thread's own start. Every later call carried it, which is why 97 percent of the context tokens were cache reads and the uncached input over the whole session was about two thousand tokens. The plan's section 4 says what that costs and what it is the first measure of.
- The time. Of 2,252 active seconds, 172 were in tools, two at once counted once, and 2,081 outside them, the model's and the harness's; the harness counted 989 seconds of thinking. The 142,792 output tokens over those 2,081 seconds are about 69 tokens a second, which is the model writing: this thread's time went to its own output, thinking and text, and not to its tools. Per tool the seconds are summed, so the shell's 468 seconds against the 172-second union say its calls mostly ran beside other calls.
- The reading. 45 calls named 68 files, 46 of them distinct, 11 of them files the session had read before; 31 calls read part of a file (head, tail, a sed range), the auto-mode instruction to read through the shell at work, and 95 shell commands were filters on another command's output. Of the 450 KB the reads returned, 126 KB came back from calls that only re-read, about 28 percent, which is the most a hashing index (the plan's section 2, last row) could have saved this session before its own cost is counted.
- The context. It grew by 190,233 tokens over the session, 149,880 of them attributed to the shell, whose 101 calls returned 525 KB; the rest to the notifications, the web and the tool search.
- The failures. Three of the eight failed calls were WebFetch calls to hosts this environment cannot resolve (dev.to, grith.ai, web.archive.org); two were shell commands, a wrong output path and a syntax slip, found and fixed in the same minute; two were reads of files that are not on the branch, and one a reviewer request GitHub refuses on one's own pull request. The one unanswered call is the command that took this profile, whose answer was not yet written when it read the file.

What a baseline of one session cannot say: whether these figures are typical of the project's threads, which the profile trailer of the plan's step 1 answers over every commit, or what they would be under any arm of section 2, which is what the arms are for. And what this record cannot do: be made again, since the transcript it was read from lives in the session's container and is not kept; the tool's tests are the reproducible part, and a kept transcript (coordbench's harness keeps one per run) gives the first baseline that can be regenerated.
