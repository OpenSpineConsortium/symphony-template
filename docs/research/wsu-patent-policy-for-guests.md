# WSU's patent policy and inventors who are not WSU employees

Source: the thread "Review your rights to the SchwingNet invention", 2026-10-24, a review of Wayne State University's Patent and Copyright Policy (Board statute 2.41.04, last amended 13 July 1984, read as the Technology Transfer Office's copy of 6/2/1998 and at bog.wayne.edu/code/2-41-04) and of WSU's public pages on Guest AccessIDs, the Grid and acceptable use. It is the general policy analysis only: a factor list, not a legal opinion, and nothing here was checked by counsel. The ip-legal plugin's case law servers did not connect from the cloud session, so the case law named is from memory and tagged to verify. The owner's own contract and the facts of the invention are kept in a private memo in the project's files, not here.

## Who the policy reaches

The preamble says the policy "is applicable to all full-time and fractional-time University employees", who sign a Patent Agreement, and procedure 86-5 puts the duty to report inventions on employees alone. But section 2.2 reaches further: WSU holds "right of first refusal to hold title to all patentable inventions made by non-salaried personnel or other individuals affiliated with the University, if such inventions are made with support of University funds, facilities, or equipment". The definitions name hourly, part-time, volunteer, adjunct, cooperating and visiting faculty and undergraduate, graduate and postdoctoral students, and say the list is "not limited to" them.

So the trigger for a non-employee is University support, not employment. Section 2.1 defines support as "salary, funds, facilities, equipment or services not available to the general public". The Grid is such equipment, and a Guest AccessID exists only on a full-time WSU employee's sponsorship, so a guest who trains a model on the Grid fits the words of section 2.2 even though nothing in the preamble or the procedure binds them.

## What the claim is

Section 2.2 is a right of first refusal and a promise that title "will be assigned", not a present transfer. Under US law an invention belongs to its inventor until the inventor assigns it in writing (Stanford v. Roche, 2011; FilmTec v. Allied-Signal, 1991; from memory, verify), so WSU's claim against someone who never signed a patent agreement rests on whether the policy is a contract they assented to. That is the open question for counsel. The common-law shop right belongs to an employer, which WSU is not for a guest. WSU must elect or waive within four calendar months of a report of invention; for an invention it agrees was made without support, it acknowledges the inventor's ownership in writing within 20 working days (section 2.1). Disputes go to WSU's own Patent Committee (section 5.0).

## What strengthens it

Three facts turn a guest-and-equipment argument into a strong one: conception while the inventor was a WSU student, who is named in the definitions; a WSU employee contributing to the conception of a claim, whose share WSU takes, making WSU a co-owner; and WSU-administered or federal funds, which bring section 2.3 and Bayh-Dole. Separately, WSU's acceptable use policy (2000) forbids "using University information technology resources for personal for-profit purposes".

## If WSU takes title

WSU pays the patent costs, the inventor keeps 75 % of the first 10,000 USD of net royalty, then 50 %, 40 % and 35 % (section 4.0), title returns to the inventor if WSU has not licensed within 24 months of issue (section 3.0), and an exclusive licence, ten years renewable for seven, may go to an employee's company (section 3.1).

## On joining the faculty

From the start date the policy binds the inventor as an employee, and section 2.1 asks that an employee's own invention be initiated and perfected "outside normal University service", which an appointment in the invention's field rarely allows. A pre-existing invention is best settled, by WSU's written waiver or acknowledgment, and listed as excluded in the hiring paperwork before the offer is signed.

## What to confirm

Whether a newer WSU IP policy, a student IP policy or Grid account terms apply (this statute predates Stanford v. Roche); whether the inventor ever signed a WSU patent agreement as a student or research assistant; and a section 2.2 waiver from WSU Technology Commercialization, with patent counsel reading the report of invention first. A patent application may be filed in the inventor's name before ownership is settled.

## Federal fellowships and WSU's standard MOU

An individual Kirschstein-NRSA fellowship (an F30, F31 or F32) brings no federal rights: 35 U.S.C. 212 bars a fellowship made "primarily to an awardee for educational purposes" from giving the agency "any rights to inventions made by the awardee", and the NIH Grants Policy Statement, 11.2.13.5, puts such fellowships outside 37 CFR 401 and outside invention reporting (read 2026-10-24; verify). What the fellowship does do is place the fellow at WSU as a student, which section 2.2 names.

WSU's invention disclosure form (version of 1/25/2024) carries the Memorandum of Understanding on its pages 11 to 13. It is a present assignment, "the Inventors hereby assign to the University all their right, title, and interest in and to the subject invention(s)", of an invention it never defines. Its reversion (clause 7) runs only from 24 months "following issuance of any corresponding patent", so it never runs if WSU does not file. Its commitment to file is "if warranted". Its clause 8 makes the 1984 statute binding "other provisions of this Memorandum notwithstanding". Before signing, an inventor would reasonably ask for a schedule defining the subject invention, a reversion that also runs if WSU does not file, a filing date, and, for a founder, an option to an exclusive licence. The form's own signature page asks department and dean signatures of "WSU Faculty and other Employees".

## Filing, inventorship and the policy's clocks

Added 2026-10-24 from the same thread. A provisional application needs no inventor's signature,
so the university's counsel can file one without the inventor; a later full application needs
the inventor's declaration, or a substitute statement from a party that shows a proprietary
interest (37 CFR 1.46 and 1.64; from memory, verify). Filing does not move ownership: the inventor
holds title until a written assignment (35 U.S.C. 261), and an applicant or assignee named on the
cover sheet or data sheet is the place an ownership claim enters the record, so an inventor who
means to keep title asks to be named applicant with no assignee. Inventorship is a separate fact:
only those who conceived the claims are inventors, a university never is, and a wrongly added
co-inventor is the real risk, since each co-owner may license without the others (35 U.S.C. 262).
The policy itself sets two clocks from the report of invention: a written determination within
twenty working days under section 2.1, and a decision to acquire or waive within four calendar
months under section 2.2. It does not say what silence means. Its definition of University
support includes "facilities, equipment or services not available to the general public", which
reads on a research computing cluster, so a guest's strongest points lie outside the policy's
words: that they never assented to it and that section 2.2 promises an assignment rather than
making one.
