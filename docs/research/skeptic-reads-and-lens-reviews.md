# Skeptic reads and lens reviews of research in a private repository

The thread "SchwingNet textbook and critique reviews", 2026-10-23. The owner asked for full adversarial reviews of two pieces of research in a private repository before either merged ("not for this super important work, please do adversarial reviews in this thread, this is a new account"), on an account whose usage was not yet the limit. One piece was the contradiction candidates of the reference book synthesised from an account's artifacts (SchwingNet #136, pull request #137), whose inventory artifact-inventory-process.md beside this file describes; the other a long critique of two published approaches set against the project's own plan (SchwingNet #140, pull request #145). Both pieces, and everything they say, stay in that repository under its confidentiality rule; this note keeps the process alone: how each review was run, the kinds of error the reviewers caught, what it cost and what failed, so the next review of research is run the same way or better.

## Two shapes of review

Many short claims and one long argument fail differently, so they were reviewed differently.

### Many short claims: a skeptic per group, then a review of the skeptics

The book's candidates were statements two artifacts make differently: disagreements, revised figures, stale statements, duplicated texts and shared unsourced claims inside topic clusters, and quantities two artifacts give with different values. Earlier readers had found them and a script had looked for each quote in the source; what was missing was a reader who read both sources and said whether the candidate holds.

1. The candidates were cut into ten units, nine groups of clusters and one of number collisions, each a JSON file on disk naming, for every candidate, both artifacts with the paths of their plain text and their own files, the earlier reader's statement and quote, and what the script's quote check found. The inputs were files rather than text in the workflow's script, so a relaunched unit read exactly what the first launch read.
2. One skeptic per unit read its file whole, then for each candidate searched a distinctive fragment of each quote in the text and, failing that, in the artifact's own files, read the passage around it for the quantity, scope, denominator, version and date, and gave one verdict on a fixed scale, four values for a cluster candidate (confirmed, superseded by a later source, refuted, cannot tell) and six for a number (a confirmed contradiction, updated later, the same number restated, a scope or unit that differs, different quantities, cannot tell). Each verdict carries the exact words it rests on, copied and never paraphrased, a few sentences on what the texts show and the value the book should carry. The skeptics were read-only: no edit, no git, no network, no agent, no grid; and their brief said never to copy an e-mail address, a phone number, a street address or a URL into their output, and to name people by role.
3. A second skeptic was to be asked only for the indices the first left out, so that a long unit is finished without paying to read it twice; every unit returned all of its indices, so none was asked.
4. A script merged the verdicts into the book's record at each candidate's index, asserting that no index is filled twice, and keyed the number verdicts exactly as the renderer looks them up; a second script rendered the page and its counts from that record, so the page and the record cannot disagree.
5. Last, the verdicts were reviewed three ways, an earlier account's verifiers' among them.
   - A sample of the verdicts was read again by agents told to overturn them. The draw is by stratum, one per cluster and one for the numbers, and within each by the SHA-1 of the string "stratum|row", rows counted from 0, lowest first: from every stratum two verdicts that put a candidate on the confirmed list and one that dismissed it, then the five dismissals and the four cannot-tell verdicts with the lowest hashes left across all strata, 54 in all; and every verdict whose own quotes the script could not place, 4 more. The script that drew it is committed beside the record, and it draws the same verdicts only from the record as it stood when the sample was drawn, since the review's own rulings and the second reading of ten later moved verdicts between sides, so that commit is written beside it and the script stops when its draw differs from the one recorded. Five re-checkers read both sources of each, three for the verdicts that put a candidate on the list and two for the rest, and one of three judges ruled wherever a re-checker disagreed with a verdict or its hint. Of the random draw, 26 of 30 verdicts that put a candidate on the list held (87 %, Wilson 95 % interval 70 to 95 %) and 22 of 24 that kept one off (92 %, 74 to 98 %); 3 of the 4 drawn for an unplaced quote were overturned. All 11 rulings sided with the re-checker: 9 verdicts overturned and 2 hints corrected. Those rates are unweighted: every stratum gave the same number of verdicts whatever its size, so a small stratum counts as much as a large one. Weighted by how many verdicts each stratum holds that the review did not read, about 82 % of the 471 confirmed ones are expected to hold, and the weakest stratum, where both drawn confirmations were overturned, holds 55 of them; and cannot-tell, a third of the verdicts that kept a candidate off the list, was a sixth of the draw. The page gives each verdict as ruled, with its first verdict beside it, and the rates both ways, so a reader knows how far to trust the verdicts nobody read again and which to read first.
   - A reviewer of the pipeline re-ran it from the workflows' journals to the page: re-extracting the journals and re-running the merge reproduced the kept results and the record byte for byte, each of the 848 verdicts the skeptic units gave, of the 960 in the record, was scored against every candidate of its cluster for alignment, and every count on the page was recounted. It found the merge sound and eleven problems, six medium and five low, among them: a "confirmed" that means a disagreement for one kind of candidate and a duplicated text or a shared unsourced claim for another, counted in one column; the skeptics counting rows from 0 where the pages counted from 1; links from pages one folder deeper that pointed nowhere; a renderer whose defaults were another session's paths, so that with them unset it wrote a clean-looking page; inputs kept only in a scratchpad; a quote check that compared spacing; and ten verdicts given while the plain text cut each line of a page's script data at 600 characters, where their values were. All were fixed, and the ten were read again in the artifacts' own files.
   - A privacy reader went through every string of every file the pull request carried and cross-checked them against the addresses, numbers and names the sources print. It found a person's first name, a login in a path, a network range, a form's short link, a staff member's title at a named employer, a settings page's address and another group's folder name, which a script replaced with an editor's words in square brackets that the quote check matches around; and it found that the replacement of e-mail addresses and phone numbers the notes described had been done outside the extraction script, which now does it.

### One long argument: lenses, then a skeptic for each finding

A long note fails by its argument as much as by its facts, so it was read by four lenses, each in an agent of its own: the papers at their source (the arXiv version each number comes from, and the released code where it settled a point), the repository's own records, fairness to both research groups and to the project alike, and whether the note answers each of the issue's asks. Each lens returned findings with the place, the problem, the evidence and a fix, and every finding went to a skeptic told to refute it, which answered confirmed, partly (with a narrower fix) or refuted.

The four lenses raised 47 findings: the skeptics confirmed 26 as written, held 20 in part and refuted 1, which had cited one version of a paper's dataset against a note that quoted the later version correctly. Of the 46 that held, 5 were high, 23 medium and 18 low, and three of the five high ones were one point seen by three lenses, so the most serious point did not rest on one lens. All 46 were applied, each in the form the skeptic left it where the skeptic narrowed it, and a second reader then checked the rewrite against the list of fixes and the sources. The second reader found every fix applied as the skeptic left it, and ten problems and eleven smaller ones that the rewrite had kept or brought in: nine of them came in with the fixes themselves, six of those from fixes applied as a skeptic had narrowed them. Among them were a comparator in a paper's table the note had not seen, a figure from one case read as a general result, a comparison that handed one side the answer, a timing set beside one that measures something else, and a step written as run that is only written. Each was checked again against the sources before it went in, and all held; one point, which only a record in a repository the session could not read would settle, is left open in the note.

## A follow-up: one side read whole, then its siblings ruled together

The sample's weakest stratum had both of its drawn confirmations overturned, so the owner asked for every verdict on the confirmed side of that stratum that the sample had left out to be read again: 55 verdicts, the whole side rather than a sample of it.

1. A script selected them from the record as the review's pull request merged it and wrote five input files, every fifth row to each, so that no re-checker read a run of neighbouring rows. Each re-checker was told to overturn each verdict and its hint, as the review's were, and a judge ruled on each of the 20 disputes. The brief differed from the review's in three ways the page states: five re-checkers in place of three; a note marking a page that had changed after the inventory read it; and one sentence more, which told the re-checkers that the review had overturned both such verdicts it drew. That sentence is a prior toward overturning, so the follow-up's rates are given apart from the review's and never pooled with them.
2. An adversarial reviewer of that reading re-ran it from the journal to the page and found the mechanics sound: the selection, the join, the outcomes and the render all reproduced. What it found wrong was in the rulings. Rows that read one figure, or the same two pages, had gone to different re-checkers and judges, and gave a planner different instructions for one quantity, the most important figure of the set among them; one ruling rested on the premise that the later pages consistently carried a figure, which a page of the same date did not; and two rulings took a candidate off the confirmed list on a scope the readers had computed, a sum of one page's counts that matched another page's total, where the verdict scale asks for a scope the pages state. The page also counted a contradiction that became a later revision as not held, though it stayed on the list, set intervals on the whole side without saying what they describe, and kept, in an unweighted sentence, draws from the stratum it had now read whole.
3. So the rows were ruled again by group: one judge for each of five groups of rows that read the same figures or the same pages, 15 rows in all. Each judge was shown every row of its group with its verdict and hint as they then stood, the reviewer's points about them, the further sources the reviewer named, and any row the review itself had ruled on the same figures, set beside them for comparison and given a note, not ruled again. Of the 15, 3 kept their verdict and hint and 12 were ruled again. A script applied the group rulings after the single ones, so each row reads as the last reader to change it has it, with its outcome set against the verdict the follow-up read; and the two workflows' scripts were committed beside the tools that read their journals.
4. In all 41 of the 55 held, 6 of them with a corrected hint, and 14 were overturned; 46 stay on the confirmed list and 9 left it. Since every row was read, the counts are exact, and the page now says its intervals describe how well such readers' rate of error is known, not how many of these rows may be wrong.
5. Siblings live across strata too. Verdicts of another stratum carry some of the same figures from the same pages, read by their own skeptics and never with the groups, and some of them plainly carry what a group's judge found the texts do not support. Ruling them with the groups would be a pass of its own, so the renderer finds them by a rule the page states, a row whose quotes give one of a group's figures, written the same way, and each of whose pages the group's judge read, or two of the figures and one such page, and marks each of the 45 it finds to be read beside its group's finding.
6. A second adversarial reader of the whole branch re-ran every step from the kept inputs and journals to the page and found each reproduced byte for byte. What it found was again in the rulings, and in what the page left unsaid. One group's judge had ruled two rows a later revision while its own note said no page settles whether the two figures measure one thing, and a page of the same date still gave the earlier figure, which the judges' own scale says bears on whether it was replaced. The judges' scale carried three rules the re-checkers' did not, which decided two rows, and the page did not quote them. The rows of other strata were marked to be read beside a finding, though several of them plainly carried what the finding rejected; the marking rule took a group's figures from its rows' values only, missing the very figures that named two groups, and its sentence on the page said less than its code; and where a judge sided with the skeptic in other words, the words were dropped when the ruling was applied. It also proposed a caveat on one figure that the project's owner had settled is never to be raised; that one was declined, with the owner's decision cited.
7. So three groups were ruled a second time, one judge each, with that reader's points checked against the texts before the judges were given them, and two rules added to the brief: never copy a login or a grid path, and leave out what the owner has settled. The page now quotes the rules each round's brief added, and a tool refuses to write a round's account unless the workflow still gives each rule word for word. The marking rule counts a group's quantities as well as its values, and finds 49 rows where it found 45. Each marked row was then set beside its group's finding as it stood after the second round, by the group's judge or by a reader given that finding, and the row's mark now says whether its instruction agrees with the finding, differs from it, with what to correct, or is about another quantity. Of the 13 rows ruled a second time, 2 kept their verdict and hint and 11 were ruled again; the two rows the reader had questioned became cannot-tell, both figures carried side by side with the page that gives each and the record named that would settle them, while the rows beside them that the later pages revise with nothing left to doubt stayed revisions. In all 41 of the 55 held, 9 of them with a corrected hint, and 14 were overturned; 44 stay on the confirmed list and 11 left it. Of the 49 marked rows, 24 agree with their group's finding, 20 differ and 5 are about another quantity, and the page lists the 20 with what each note says. Every step of the round reproduced from the kept files: its inputs written again from the reading as it now stands, the join of its journal into the reading as it stood before, the record and the render, each byte for byte.
8. A third adversarial reader re-ran every step again and found the mechanics reproduced and the rulings it questioned right on the texts. What it found was in the marking rule and in what the page and the records left unsaid. The rule needed each of a row's pages to be one a group's judge read, or two of the group's figures and one such page, so rows of other strata that carried one of a finding's figures on a page read beside the group, by the second round's judges or readers, or on one page the judge read beside another it did not, went unmarked, two of them plainly carrying what a finding says not to carry. A change in how a ruling that sided with the skeptic in other words is applied, now a corrected hint where the skeptic's words had been kept, moved two rows from held to hint corrected with nothing said. A mark showed its note only where the row differs, though notes that agree carried qualifications a planner needs. The account of the judges' scale quoted three added rules where the scale added four sentences and a gloss on one verdict. And the thread's own summaries credited a figure to two pages where one gives it and the other gives its parts. So the rule now also counts the pages of the rows read beside a group, and marks a row that gives one of the figures on one page read for the group: it added 26 rows and dropped none, and one reader for each group, under the second round's brief word for word with one sentence added to its account of the work, set each beside the finding as it now stands; 3 agree, 2 differ, the two the reader found, and 21 are about another quantity, which their notes say. Every mark now shows its note, the page names the two rows the counting change moved, the scale's account quotes every added sentence, each checked against the workflows' scripts, and each row's review cell names the rounds that changed it. The whole chain was then re-run from the reading as the re-check left it, through the three journals, and the reading, the record and the pages came out byte for byte.
9. A fourth adversarial reader re-ran every step and found each reproduced. What it found was in the marking rule again, in the words of a few notes and hints, and in the thread's own accounts. The rule read a row's figures in its quotes alone, so a confirmed row whose verdict's explanation and hint give a group's figures, and tell a planner which to carry, went unmarked; and it took any run of two digits as a figure, so rows were marked on the digits inside a label, the minutes of a timestamp or a price. Three notes and two hints misstated a page or what a finding credits to it, one of them a note from the second round that no reader questioned until every note was shown; two notes gave one instruction two words; and the commit message, the resume notes and the pull request's body gave the wrong reason why the narrow rule had missed rows. So the rule now also reads each verdict's explanation and hint, takes a figure only where it stands alone, not joined to a letter, a colon or a date, and needs two where each a row shares is a whole number under 100: it unmarked 14 rows, all of them noted as about another quantity, whose notes stay in the reading and leave the page, and added 5, which one reader for each of their two groups set beside the finding as it now stands, under the third pass's brief with one sentence added; 4 agree and 1 differs. The notes and hints that misstate a page keep their readers' words, and the editor's words follow each in square brackets, naming the page they rest on, kept in a file of their own and put in the reading by a script, which the renderer checks land on a hint or a note the page shows. One point, that two lines settle which measure a figure is, was weighed against the texts and left, since neither says. The reading was then joined again from the five workflows' journals, byte for byte, and each round's inputs were written again from it. Of the 66 rows of other strata now marked, 31 agree with their group's finding, 23 differ and 12 are about another quantity.

## The kinds of error the reviewers caught

These are the kinds, without the research they were found in.

- A capability of a baseline credited to the method under study, where one column of a paper's table pools several methods' runs.
- One version of a paper quoted with another version's figures or data, where the versions differ.
- A small figure from a paper's text quoted as its result, where its table gives different per-item figures.
- What one experiment did written as what the whole approach requires.
- A comparison called like for like when one side had help the other lacked.
- Evidence against a competitor drawn from a different system, built and scored differently.
- A criticism of a competitor that applies to the project too, left unsaid.
- In the book's candidates: two statements over different subsets, denominators or dates read as one quantity, a number from a page's script or data read as its text, and a revised figure called a contradiction when the later page names the earlier one as its baseline.
- In the skeptics' verdicts: a difference called a stated scope where neither page states one; a disagreement or a revision confirmed between figures that measure different things or count different subsets; a revision called a disagreement where the later page plainly follows the earlier; a dismissal that read only the candidate's own examples and missed a record on the same page that settles it; a cannot-tell that missed the later page doing what the skeptic said no page did; confirmations resting on a page extracted one character to a line, which read in context agrees; and a hint that called one page the earlier with nothing to show it.
- In the follow-up's rulings: rows that read one figure ruled apart and disagreeing; a premise that the later pages consistently say one thing, false for one of them; a scope a reader computed, a sum that reconciles two pages, taken for a scope a page states; a revision ruled where the judge's own finding said no page settles whether the two figures are one measure; a figure credited to a page that does not give it; one count of one set read two ways by two rows; and, in the rates, a row that changed its verdict but stayed on the list counted as not held; and, on the page, a rule for related rows drawn too narrow to catch two that contradicted a finding, then one that read a row's quotes alone and took the digits of labels, times and prices for figures; notes shown only where a row differs; notes and hints that misstate a page or what a finding credits to it; a change in the counting and an added sentence of the scale left unsaid; and an account that gave the wrong reason for what a rule had missed.

## What failed

- The container restarted while the skeptic workflow had nine of its ten units back and one running, and killed the running unit and a background agent with it. The workflow's journal (journal.jsonl in its transcript folder) held every finished result: a started entry gives each agent's key and label and a result entry its value under the same key, so a short script kept the nine results beside the record, and the tenth unit was relaunched alone from its input file. The background agent was resumed by sending it a message, which kept its context. Write each result out of the journal as soon as it lands, and keep every input in a file, so a restart costs only the units still running.
- The journal is JSON written with no space after the colon, so a grep for the spaced form counts nothing; it is counted with a JSON parser. The agents' transcripts are the same, and they, not the tools' summary figure, say what an agent spent.
- Git in a cloud session commits as the session's own default identity unless told otherwise, and a plain merge is the commit most easily missed: one from another thread reached a default branch that way. Each merge commit here was amended with the owner's identity and the session's trailers before it was pushed, or the merge was run with that identity passed by -c and its message, trailers included, from a file.
- The skeptics' brief named e-mail addresses, phone numbers, street addresses and URLs, and said to name people by role, and the privacy reader still found seven kinds of detail in the records. Nearly all of it was in the earlier readers' quotes, which the records had carried from the start, and in an earlier account's verdicts; of this review's skeptics' verdicts it found only a staff member's title, copied from a reader's quote, which naming people by role does not forbid. A brief is no screen, and a narrow one less: what every reader wrote down is screened after them, for more kinds than any brief names, a login in a path and a network range among them.
- A page extracted one character to a line defeats the quote check and the reader alike. The check now matches with spacing ignored, but 3 of the 4 verdicts drawn because their quotes could not be placed were wrong, so a verdict resting on such a page is read again.
- The sample's script was at first kept only in a scratchpad, and the description of its draw left out the string it hashed and that the extra draws came from all strata at once, so the page's claim that the draw could be repeated did not hold until the script was committed with the commit it draws from.
- curl to arxiv.org is refused by the proxy while the session's web fetch tool reads the same paper once its address has appeared in a search result or a message, as benchmark-and-priors-issues.md beside this file records; the papers lens and the skeptics read every arXiv version that way.
- The follow-up shared its rows out by position, so a re-checker or a judge saw one row of a set that read one figure and could not keep it in line with the others; one group re-rule, five judges, put that right after the review found it. Grouping the rows that read one figure, or the same two pages, before the reading, and giving each group to one reader and one judge, costs nothing more and needs no second pass.
- A sentence in a brief that tells the readers what an earlier review found changes the instrument: the follow-up's readers were told the review had overturned both such verdicts it drew, so their rate of overturning cannot be set beside the review's as one measure. Leave such a sentence out, or state it beside the rates, as the page now does.
- A fix is a new claim. Where a skeptic narrowed a finding, the narrowed form was applied, and in one case the skeptic showed that the finding's first suggested replacement was as weak as what it replaced; but the second reader found that the fixes, narrowed or not, brought in nine of its problems, among them a narrowed sentence that was false, an aside copied from a skeptic's reasoning into the note and a fix whose own citation pointed to the wrong section.
- A judge's verdict can outrun its own finding. One group's judge wrote that no page settles whether two figures measure one thing and then ruled them a revision of one another, which its scale gives only for one quantity, and nothing caught it until the next reader set the verdicts beside the note. Read each judge's verdicts against its own finding before they are applied.
- An instrument drifts between rounds unless it is pinned. The group judges' scale carried four sentences and a gloss on one verdict that the re-checkers' did not, two rows turned on them, and the page did not say so until the second reader asked, and then quoted three of the sentences until the third reader counted; the second round's brief added two more. A tool now writes each round's account with every added sentence quoted, and refuses to unless the workflow's script still gives each word for word. A check that what is quoted is in the script does not show that nothing was left out, so the account is now checked against both scripts, for the sentences the later one adds and for what the earlier one lacked.
- A rule for finding the related rows of other strata is a claim like any other. It was drawn narrow, each of a row's pages one the judge read or two of the group's figures, so as not to flood the page, and it missed rows that plainly bear on a finding; widened to one figure on one page read for the group, it brought in 26 more, most of them about another quantity, which their notes say. A wide rule with a note on every row it marks costs a pass of readers but leaves nothing unsaid; a narrow one costs a review to find what it missed.
- A rule that reads figures as runs of digits finds digits: the digits inside a label, the minutes of a timestamp and the price of a plan all matched a group's figures. A figure is a number standing alone, a lone small whole number is too common to mark a row by itself, and a verdict's explanation and hint are as much the row's words as its quotes, so a rule that reads the quotes alone misses the row that tells a planner what to carry.
- A reader's words that no one is shown are never checked. Once every note was on the page, the next reader found a note from two rounds before that a page contradicts. A misstated note or hint is corrected after it, in square brackets that name the page, rather than rewritten, so the page keeps both what the reader wrote and what the text says, and the correction is checked to land on a note the page shows.
- An account of why a rule missed something is a claim too. The thread's commit message, resume notes and pull request body all gave one reason for rows a rule had missed, true of one row and false of the others, and only a reader who recounted each row against the rule's code found it. Say why a row was missed by the rule's own terms, row by row, and count the rows each term kept out.
- A workflow's script cannot read files, though its agents can, so a round's standing words go to its agents as the paths of the files a script wrote, not as text in the workflow's arguments, and the join checks each kept verdict against that file.

## What to keep

Check every quote by script before a skeptic reads it, and have the skeptic copy its quotes rather than restate them. Keep the verdict scale fixed and merge by script at the index, with an assertion. Review the reviewers on a deterministic sample that holds both the verdicts that kept a candidate and those that dismissed one, read again by agents told to overturn them, with a judge where the two disagree; commit the script that draws it with the commit it draws from, and give its rates weighted by stratum as well as plain, since equal quotas make a small stratum count as much as a large one. For a long argument, prefer several lenses to one reader, a skeptic for every finding before anything changes, the narrowed fix over the first one, and a second reader of the rewrite, since every fix is a new claim to check against the sources. Re-run the merge and the render from the kept inputs and compare the bytes, so a review can trust that the page is the record, and make every tool stop on a missing path, an index it cannot place or a word off its scale rather than write a clean-looking page. Before a reading, group the rows that read one figure or the same pages and give each group to one reader and one judge, look for the same figures in the other strata, and mark what was not read with them; keep from the brief anything that tells the readers what to expect, or state it beside the rates; and say of a census that its counts are exact.
