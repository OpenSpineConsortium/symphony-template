# Skeptic reads and lens reviews of research in a private repository

The thread "SchwingNet textbook and Flepp critiques", 2026-10-23. The owner asked for full adversarial reviews of two pieces of research in a private repository before either merged ("not for this super important work, please do adversarial reviews in this thread, this is a new account"), on an account whose usage was not yet the limit. One piece was the contradiction candidates of the reference book synthesised from an account's artifacts (SchwingNet #136, pull request #137), whose inventory artifact-inventory-process.md beside this file describes; the other a long critique of two published approaches set against the project's own plan (SchwingNet #140, pull request #145). Both pieces, and everything they say, stay in that repository under its confidentiality rule; this note keeps the process alone: how each review was run, the kinds of error the reviewers caught, what it cost and what failed, so the next review of research is run the same way or better.

## Two shapes of review

Many short claims and one long argument fail differently, so they were reviewed differently.

### Many short claims: a skeptic per group, then a review of the skeptics

The book's candidates were statements two artifacts make differently: disagreements, revised figures, stale statements, duplicated texts and shared unsourced claims inside topic clusters, and quantities two artifacts give with different values. Earlier readers had found them and a script had looked for each quote in the source; what was missing was a reader who read both sources and said whether the candidate holds.

1. The candidates were cut into ten units, nine groups of clusters and one of number collisions, each a JSON file on disk naming, for every candidate, both artifacts with the paths of their plain text and their own files, the earlier reader's statement and quote, and what the script's quote check found. The inputs were files rather than text in the workflow's script, so a relaunched unit read exactly what the first launch read.
2. One skeptic per unit read its file whole, then for each candidate searched a distinctive fragment of each quote in the text and, failing that, in the artifact's own files, read the passage around it for the quantity, scope, denominator, version and date, and gave one verdict on a fixed scale, four values for a cluster candidate (confirmed, superseded by a later source, refuted, cannot tell) and six for a number (a confirmed contradiction, updated later, the same number restated, a scope or unit that differs, different quantities, cannot tell). Each verdict carries the exact words it rests on, copied and never paraphrased, a few sentences on what the texts show and the value the book should carry. The skeptics were read-only: no edit, no git, no network, no agent, no grid; no address of any kind copied out; people by role.
3. A second skeptic was asked only for the indices the first left out, so a long unit is finished without paying to read it twice.
4. A script merged the verdicts into the book's record at each candidate's index, asserting that no index is filled twice, and keyed the number verdicts exactly as the renderer looks them up; a second script rendered the page and its counts from that record, so the page and the record cannot disagree.
5. Last, the skeptics themselves were reviewed, three ways.
   - A sample of the verdicts was read again by agents told to overturn them. The draw is deterministic: within each stratum (each cluster, and the numbers) the rows with the lowest SHA-1 of stratum and row, two verdicts that put a candidate on the confirmed list and one that kept a candidate off it, then five more that kept one off and four cannot-tell, 54 in all; and every verdict whose own quotes the script could not place, 4 more. Five re-checkers read both sources of each, three for the verdicts that put a candidate on the list and two for the rest, and one of three judges ruled wherever a re-checker disagreed with a verdict or its hint. Of the random draw, 26 of 30 verdicts that put a candidate on the list held (87 %, Wilson 95 % interval 70 to 95 %) and 22 of 24 that kept one off (92 %, 74 to 98 %); 3 of the 4 drawn for an unplaced quote were overturned. All 11 rulings sided with the re-checker: 9 verdicts overturned and 2 hints corrected. The page gives each verdict as ruled, with its first verdict beside it, and the rates, so a reader knows how far to trust the verdicts nobody read again.
   - A reviewer of the pipeline re-ran it from the workflows' journals to the page: re-extracting the journals and re-running the merge reproduced the kept results and the record byte for byte, every verdict was scored against every candidate of its cluster for alignment, and every count on the page was recounted. It found the merge sound and eleven problems, six medium and five low: a "confirmed" that means a disagreement for one kind of candidate and a duplicated text or a shared unsourced claim for another, counted in one column; the skeptics counting rows from 0 where the pages counted from 1; links from pages one folder deeper that pointed nowhere; a renderer whose defaults were another session's paths, so that with them unset it wrote a clean-looking page; inputs kept only in a scratchpad; a quote check that compared spacing; and ten verdicts given while the plain text cut each line of a page's script data at 600 characters, where their values were. All were fixed, and the ten were read again in the artifacts' own files.
   - A privacy reader went through every string of every file the pull request carried and cross-checked them against the addresses, numbers and names the sources print. It found a person's first name, a login in a path, a network range, a form's short link, a staff member's title at a named employer, a settings page's address and another group's folder name, which a script replaced with an editor's words in square brackets that the quote check matches around; and it found that the replacement of e-mail addresses and phone numbers the notes described had been done outside the extraction script, which now does it.

### One long argument: lenses, then a skeptic for each finding

A long note fails by its argument as much as by its facts, so it was read by four lenses, each in an agent of its own: the papers at their source (the arXiv version each number comes from, and the released code where it settled a point), the repository's own records, fairness to both research groups and to the project alike, and whether the note answers each of the issue's asks. Each lens returned findings with the place, the problem, the evidence and a fix, and every finding went to a skeptic told to refute it, which answered confirmed, partly (with a narrower fix) or refuted.

The four lenses raised 47 findings: the skeptics confirmed 26 as written, held 20 in part and refuted 1, which had cited one version of a paper's dataset against a note that quoted the later version correctly. Of the 46 that held, 5 were high, 23 medium and 18 low, and three of the five high ones were one point seen by three lenses, which is the case for lenses over one reviewer read three times. All 46 were applied, each in the form the skeptic left it where the skeptic narrowed it, and a second reader then checked the rewrite against the list of fixes and the sources. The second reader found every fix applied as the skeptic left it, and ten problems and eleven smaller ones that the rewrite had kept or brought in: among them a comparator in a paper's table the note had not seen, a figure from one image read as a general result, a like-for-like comparison that handed the bodies' locations to one side only, the time of one part of a pipeline set beside another system's whole, and a step written as run that is only written. Each was checked again against the sources before it went in, and all held; one point, which only a record in a repository the session could not read would settle, is left open in the note.

## The kinds of error the reviewers caught

These are the kinds, without the research they were found in.

- A capability of a baseline credited to the method under study, where one column of a paper's table holds several methods' runs under a heading that names every prompt any of them took.
- One version of a paper quoted with another version's numbers, dataset or test specimen, when the versions differ in all three.
- A small figure from a paper's text quoted as its result, where its table gives the per-item figures, several times larger.
- What one experiment did, an angle or an extra instrument, written as what the whole approach requires.
- A help the project's own evaluation gets at test, unstated, held against a competitor that takes the same help as a prompt.
- Evidence against a competitor's regime drawn from the project's own system, built and scored differently.
- The project's truth made by the same kind of tool the note criticised the competitor for leaning on.
- In the book's candidates: two statements over different subsets, denominators or dates read as one quantity, a number from a page's script or data read as its text, and a revised figure called a contradiction when the later page names the earlier one as its baseline.
- In the skeptics' verdicts: a difference called a stated scope where neither page states one; a disagreement or a revision confirmed between figures that measure different things or count different subsets; a revision called a disagreement where the later page plainly follows the earlier; a dismissal that read only the candidate's own examples and missed a record on the same page that settles it; a cannot-tell that missed the later page doing what the skeptic said no page did; confirmations resting on a page extracted one character to a line, which read in context agrees; and a hint that called one page the earlier with nothing to show it.

Tokens as the session's tools reported them for each agent or workflow, and the time each took:

|---|---|---|---|
| The critique's four lenses and a skeptic per finding | 51 | 4,719,095 | 37 |
| The critique's second reader | 1 | 304,919 | 42 |
| The book's skeptics, nine units | 9 | not recorded | - |
| The book's skeptics, the tenth unit relaunched | 1 | 291,327 | 14 |
| The review of the skeptics: five re-checkers and three judges | 8 | 1,304,866 | 24 |
| A first launch of that review, stopped after twenty seconds | 1 | 100,911 | 0 |
| The reviewer of the pipeline | 1 | 358,501 | 26 |
| The privacy reader | 1 | 196,512 | 35 |

The recorded steps come to about 7.6 million tokens: about 5.0 million for the critique, about 2.3 million for reviewing the book's skeptics, and 0.3 million for the relaunched unit. The nine units' workflow was killed by a container restart before it wrote its record, so their cost is not known. Both reviews changed what a planner would carry: in the critique 46 applied findings and 21 more from the second reader, and in the book 9 overturned verdicts and 2 corrected hints, with error rates for the verdicts nobody read again.

## What failed

- The container restarted while the skeptic workflow had nine of its ten units back and one running, and killed the running unit and a background agent with it. The workflow's journal (journal.jsonl in its transcript folder) held every finished result: a started entry gives each agent's key and label and a result entry its value under the same key, so a short script kept the nine results beside the record, and the tenth unit was relaunched alone from its input file. The background agent was resumed by sending it a message, which kept its context. Write each result out of the journal as soon as it lands, and keep every input in a file, so a restart costs only the units still running.
- The journal is JSON written with no space after the colon, so a grep for the spaced form counts nothing; it is counted with a JSON parser.
- A plain git merge in a cloud session commits as the session's own default identity, not the owner's; each merge commit was amended with the owner's identity and the session's trailers before it was pushed, or the merge was run with that identity passed by -c and its message, trailers included, from a file.
- The skeptics' brief said to copy out no address of any kind and to name people by role, and their verdicts still carried a grid path that names a login and a staff member's title; most of what the privacy reader found came from the earlier readers' quotes. A brief is no screen: what the readers and skeptics wrote down is screened after them.
- A page extracted one character to a line defeats the quote check and the reader alike. The check now matches with spacing ignored, but 3 of the 4 verdicts drawn because their quotes could not be placed were wrong, so a verdict resting on such a page is read again.
- python3 -I ignores every PYTHON variable, PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE among them, so a tool run with -I writes __pycache__ beside itself unless -B is passed too.
- curl to arxiv.org is refused by the proxy while the session's web fetch tool reads the same paper once its address has appeared in a search result or a message, as benchmark-and-priors-issues.md beside this file records; the papers lens and the skeptics read every arXiv version that way.
- A finding's fix is not always the right fix: where a skeptic narrowed one, the narrowed form was applied, and in one case the skeptic showed that the finding's first suggested replacement was as weak as what it replaced.

## What to keep

Check every quote by script before a skeptic reads it, and have the skeptic copy its quotes rather than restate them. Keep the verdict scale fixed and merge by script at the index, with an assertion. Review the reviewers on a deterministic sample that holds both the verdicts that kept a candidate and those that dismissed one, read again by agents told to overturn them, with a judge where the two disagree. For a long argument, prefer several lenses to one reader, a skeptic for every finding before anything changes, and the narrowed fix over the first one. Re-run the merge and the render from the kept inputs and compare the bytes, so a review can trust that the page is the record, and make every tool stop on a missing path, an index it cannot place or a word off its scale rather than write a clean-looking page. Screen what the readers and skeptics wrote, not only what they read. And treat a restart as certain: inputs in files, results out of the journal as they land.
