## 1. FleXray, from its public code and cards

Read in the fork and on the model and dataset cards (huggingface.co/VictorButoi/flexray and VictorButoi/flexray-data) and the project page (flexray.csail.mit.edu). Each fact names where it was read so the audit threads can start from it rather than read the fork again.

- One network, one head. A 2D residual UNet (src/fxr/models/unet.py), filters 64 to 1024 over seven levels, one input channel and 61 output channels under a sigmoid: 60 structures and background, the packaged model label space of the protocol all_structures_flexray_v4 (src/fxr/configs/protocols/all_structures_flexray_v4.yml, docs/protocols.md). The protocol has 63 labels; the two aggregates lumbar_spine and thoracolumbar_spine are left out of the head and declared in the eval contract as unions of vertebra channels, though the fork's own evaluator never computes the union and its training remap sends DeepFluoro's stored lumbar_spine label to background, so the paper's DeepFluoro number is not reproduced by the fork's evaluation as shipped; the project's benchmark scorer makes the union itself.
- One channel per vertebra. vertebra_c1 to c7, t1 to t12, l1 to l5 and sacrum are each a channel. There is no channel for a sixth lumbar or a thirteenth thoracic body: the MOOSE, HANSeg and ShoulderCT dataset specs list vertebra_l6, rib_left_13 and rib_right_13 under protocol_drop_labels, which compiles them to background (docs/protocols.md, "Dataset Specs"). In the standard loss route those pixels are then a hard negative for every vertebra channel, in the cross-entropy and the Dice term alike, so the released weights were trained to call a sixth lumbar body background.
- Inference at 256 pixels. Every image is padded to a square and resized to the bundle's 256 (src/fxr/inference/preprocessing.py), with no tiling and no mapping of the output back to the input grid beyond the content box; the network is fully convolutional and its public classes take another size, so a windowed helper is small (item 12). Test-time augmentation (a flip and intensity changes only) and the five-member ensemble (flux000, flux025, flux0375 the flagship, flux050, flux075; about 1.9 GiB in all) are optional, at five forward passes per augmented pass (MODEL_ZOO.md, docs/inference.md, src/fxr/inference/tta.py). A multi-frame DICOM is refused, so video is scored frame by frame. Inference scales an integer file by its dtype's range where training scaled each image by its own, which the stress test measures on 16-bit fluoroscopy frames.
- The training mix (src/fxr/configs/training/base.yml, dataloader.proportions). Ninety-six percent of every epoch is synthetic: CT volumes rendered to DRRs on the fly at 0.585 (MOOSE 3.0 at 0.375, ShoulderCT and HANSeg at 0.05 each, PedsCT and RSNAFrac at 0.04, ElbowCT at 0.03) and FluXray, the edited DRR set, at 0.375; the four real radiograph sets, hands, feet, forearms and humeri, are one percent each. Losses are routed per dataset: a SoftDice plus binary pixel cross-entropy over every channel for fully labelled sets, and a partial-label route that ignores the channels an image does not annotate (DatasetRoutedLoss, docs/losses.md). Two thousand epochs of 500 iterations, AdamW at 3e-4 with cosine decay, an EMA of the weights, exactly one GPU and one process (no distributed training), Weights and Biases required as a package but honoured in offline or disabled mode (docs/training.md, docs/experiment.md, the launch and experiment code). No file states the released run's hardware or duration.
- Where the per-vertebra supervision comes from. Every per-vertebra label the model learns is a CT label projected into a DRR (MOOSE, HANSeg and ShoulderCT from C1 to L5 and the sacrum, RSNAFrac from C1 to T7; PedsCT and ElbowCT add none) or carried by FluXray, which the dataset card says was made from MOOSE CTs; no real X-ray training set in the recipe carries a per-vertebra label. DeepFluoro appears only among the evaluation sets, and its spec stores the lumbar spine as one label (src/fxr/configs/datasets/DeepFluoro.yml, the eval contract). No spine fluoroscopy set, and no set named C-ArmLSpine, appears anywhere in the fork's configs, so the public benchmark's open question, whether FleXray trained on that set, is answered from the configs: it did not.
- The DRR path (docs/drr.md, docs/camera.md, src/fxr/drr/poses.py). The renderer is nanodrr (pinned at or above 0.1.5 in the train extra). The released profile samples the pose with alpha in [-180, 180] degrees and beta in [-30, 30], gamma 0, the isocenter on a random labelled structure, a source-to-detector distance of 1150 to 1250 mm, a detector pixel of 1.25 to 2.25 mm (320 to 576 mm at the detector), and a source 500 to 900 mm from the isocenter, so the magnification is 1.28 to 2.5 and the anatomy framed at the isocenter is 128 to 451 mm wide, 0.5 to 1.76 mm a pixel; a cone beam nine times in ten and an orthographic one otherwise; and a lognormal attenuation factor per label half the time, skewed so that most draws make bone denser. The image is the line integral of attenuation, its polarity flipped at random, with no noise model, scatter, intensifier distortion, collimation circle or grid, and the augmentation flips it and turns it in plane by up to 90 degrees, so the model is blind to laterality. A training render is made from one axial crop of 256 slices of a CT (the packing's crop of 512 by 512 by 256), so how much of a column one render shows depends on the slice spacing. The fixed presets are frontal, lateral, lateral_left, offangle (45 degrees) and above (45 degrees of tilt). The pose geometry was read in nanodrr 0.1.5's and roma 1.5.6's sources, the versions the fork's lock file pins.
- What the fork renders and what it does not. fxr-render writes raw DRRs with their masks and camera metadata from a packed CT set, with no weights and under the code's MIT licence (src/fxr/launch/render_cli.py, src/fxr/launch/render.py, docs/camera.md). It takes no dataset spec, so a set whose stored label names are outside the protocol, vertebra_l6 among them, is refused until the protocol names them; and its manifest records each view's pose relative to the isocenter but not the isocenter, so a render cannot be placed back in the CT's frame. The packer does not resample: a CT must be 512 by 512 in plane before it is packed as the released recipe packs. The generative edit that turns a raw DRR into a FluXray image, a FLUX.2 editing step by the paper, is not in the repository (CHANGELOG.md, 1.0.2: the FluXray generation and the ONNX export are not shipped). FluXray itself is in the dataset: 138,063 edited DRRs from 1,597 MOOSE CTs at 90 poses each, by the dataset card.
- Fine-tuning (docs/training.md, src/fxr/experiment/initialization.py). fxr-train --init-from starts from the released weights, from the Hugging Face id or from a local bundle directory with no network; without --replace-head the label list must match the 61 channels exactly and every tensor is copied; with it the head, the final 1x1 convolution alone, is discarded whole and freshly initialised at the new size, and --freeze-backbone trains that head alone. There is no row-wise copy that keeps 61 trained channels while adding two, and a model label must belong to the protocol, so adding vertebra_l6 and vertebra_t13 needs a new protocol file and a small piece of code, both the fork's work (item 12). The training path does take real X-ray frames with channel masks for only some structures, through the partial-label route, so real frames with a few level masks can supervise it.
- Licences. The code is MIT (LICENSE, pyproject.toml). The weights are CC BY-NC 4.0, research use only, not a medical device (the model card). The dataset card gives FluXray as CC BY-NC 4.0, each image inheriting its MOOSE site licence, and the redistributed real folders as CC BY 4.0, HaN-Seg as CC BY-NC-ND. nanodrr's own licence and the FLUX.2 weights' licence, by variant, are read by the audit's first thread, since the generator's rights differ from the weights' (item 15).
- The project page's claims, to be reproduced or set beside the record: mean Dice 0.875 over eight held-out sets; DeepFluoro Dice 0.916 against 0.866 for nnU-Net; a Cobb angle error of 5.13 degrees on AASCE with no failures; 2D-3D registration on 213 DeepFluoro frames at a median of 1.2 mm; "standard and non-standard acquisition angles".

## 2. The owner's two questions, answered from the code

### 2.1 Does FleXray classify individual vertebral instances?

It names levels, and it names them by class, not by instance. Each vertebra is a channel of its own, so a lateral or frontal frame comes back with a mask per level, and a body is whichever level's mask covers it. That is level naming from one frame, which is the task. What it is not is instance segmentation: nothing separates two regions that light the same channel, nothing orders the channels (an L3 above an L4 is not enforced), and there is no channel at all for the bodies that break the count, a sixth lumbar or a thirteenth thoracic. Worse than absent, those bodies were trained as background, because the CT sets that label them drop the label before training. So on a normal spine FleXray answers the level question; on a transitional one it must answer wrong, since no right answer is in its label space. The public benchmark scored it zero-shot on a held fluoroscopy set (SchwingNet's record, confidential); the companion note sets that result beside the record and says what it does and does not show.

### 2.2 The idea: FleXray's generation for anomalous spines, and the paired corpus against its weakness in angle

Three corrections to the premise, each from the fork, and then where the idea holds.

- The renderer is not short of angles. The released profile already draws the orbit angle from the full circle and the tilt from plus or minus 30 degrees, at a random isocenter, so a frontal, a lateral and every oblique in between are in the raw DRR training stream. What exists at only 90 fixed poses per CT is FluXray, the edited, realistic set. If FleXray is weak off the standard views, the weakness is in realism at those views, not in geometry, and the audit measures it by angle rather than assuming it.
- Nothing real supervises a level. Every per-vertebra label comes from CT. Real frames are four percent of training and carry no level (other anatomy), and the one real spine set, DeepFluoro, is evaluation only. The code can take real frames with masks for a few levels through its partial-label route; the recipe has none to give it. The paired corpus this project holds, a CT and real frames at known pose, is the one kind of data that can put a real frame and a per-vertebra label together, by projecting the CT's labels through the recorded pose, which is how FleXray's own DeepFluoro evaluation labels were made.
- Fluoroscopy's physics is absent, though its field is not. The renderer has no intensifier distortion, collimation circle, noise model or scatter. The anatomy it frames is 128 to 451 mm wide, so a nine-inch intensifier's field of about 14 cm sits at the small end of what FleXray trained on; a whole spine, at 700 mm or more, is beyond it.

Where the idea holds. Rendering from CTs with known, labelled anomalies is the clean route to rare-class X-rays: fxr-render takes a packed CT set once the protocol names the extra labels, needs no weights and is MIT, so its output is the project's own; the training sampler weights crops by the inverse of their labels' frequency, so a rare label added this way is drawn more often with no setting changed. For the paired sets the renders must be made at each real frame's recorded pose and placed in the CT's frame, which needs two small changes to fxr-render (item 12). The generative edit that gives FluXray its realism is not published, so the project's realism step is its own, or FluXray's images are used as they are, under their non-commercial licence. The paired sets (DeepFluoro, COBRA with its planning CT and 360 degrees of projections per case, C-ArmLSpine with its CBCT, Ljubljana) serve the angle question twice: as a measurement of FleXray per angle on real frames, and as supervision, a real frame beside a render of the same CT at the same pose. COBRA's licence is non-commercial and DeepFluoro's too, which touches item 15 only once a SchwingNet network trains on them; measuring with them is free of it.

## 4. Item 15: the licence and rights, laid out for the owner

Facts first. FleXray's code is MIT, so the fork, fxr-render and every render made with it from the project's own CTs are the project's to use and to license. FleXray's weights are CC BY-NC 4.0: a network fine-tuned from them, and any network trained on images they segmented or on FluXray's edited images (also CC BY-NC 4.0), is a derivative under that licence. The generator's realism step depends on FLUX.2, whose weights are licensed by variant; the audit's first thread reads which variants allow commercial use of outputs. SchwingNet's rule today classifies FleXray's weights as non-commercial, benchmark rows only, and only the owner loosens a class (tools/licences.py). The provisional, WSU Tech ID 26-1901, is the other side: a network the filing claims should not rest on weights the project may not license.

Three options, as #391 states them, none of which blocks the audit:

1. Train under the non-commercial class. Fine-tune from FleXray's weights and use FluXray as it is; the resulting network is research-only and stays out of the filing's claims. Fastest; it answers the scientific question and nothing more.
2. Rebuild from the published method on permissive data. Render with fxr-render (MIT) from the project's own and permissively licensed CTs, train FleXray's architecture from scratch on the project's recipe, add the rare-class channels from the start. Slowest; the result is the project's own and can be claimed and licensed.
3. Loosen the class in the owner's own words. Record the decision in SchwingNet's licence file and proceed as in option 1 for the project's internal use, accepting that the network is not licensable.

The thread recommends 2 for anything the filing or a licence will touch and 1 for the audit's own experiments, kept apart by the licence classes the workflow already enforces. The choice also decides whether the paired sets with non-commercial terms (DeepFluoro, COBRA) may supervise a SchwingNet network or only measure it. The owner may want the independent patent attorney's reading before 2 November; the facts above are what to give them.
