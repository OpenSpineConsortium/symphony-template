# FleXray's DRRs beside real X-ray corpora: the method and the count

The thread "Compare FleXray DRRs to real X-ray corpora", 2026-10-25, on the owner's ask of that day to plot FleXray's synthetic DRRs beside every held X-ray corpus, plain film and fluoroscopy, in t-SNE and other dimension-reduction maps, with the statistical tests for separation a machine-learning reviewer would expect. It belongs to the FleXray audit (SchwingNet #391; this repository's docs/research/flexray-audit-plan-2026-10-25.md). This note holds the public half: what is compared, how, and what the grid job costs. The design in full, the numbers once they exist and the project's own synthetic sets are in the private companion, SchwingNet docs/research/drr-domain-gap/README.md, which never leaves that repository.

At 04:15 UTC that day the owner asked whether the CTs must be segmented at all for FleXray's DRRs to be compared with real X-rays, and said the tests should run without the masks. ## What is compared

- Synthetic, seven render groups of 250 held CTs at four views each, all from the same CTs so that each pair changes one thing: FleXray's training recipe through the project's render blocks under the labels FleXray used (the replica); the same recipe through FleXray's own code (the reference, which the replica is held to); the recipe with its attenuation randomisation off (the physical control); the recipe under the project's production labels (the segmenter changed and nothing else); FleXray's physical render from the CT alone, with no mask read, its axial crops drawn uniformly and the isocentre the crop's centre (the labels' aim and crop choice taken away and nothing else); FluXray's raw-pose recipe, the 90 fixed poses the paper gives before its FLUX edit, through the blocks and through FleXray's primitives, neither reading a mask since that recipe aims at the volume's centre; and FluXray as shipped once fetched. The project's own synthetic sets stand beside them as the control that shares the CTs and changes only the renderer.
- Real fluoroscopy: C-ArmLSpine spot frames, DeepFluoro's vendor fluoroscopy (cadaver pelvis) and the Geneva hip phantom (one subject, so plotted and never tested).
- Real plain film: VinDr-SpineXR, BUU lumbar spine, AASCE, NHANES II scanned films, CSXA, KUB StoneX, the IEEE DataPort lateral whole-spine set, LUMOS (from its bank, where built) and the Aariz cephalograms.
- Each corpus gives at most 1,000 images, chosen person by person in a salted order with a cap a person; the frozen test persons are left out everywhere. Left out with reasons: web video clips (their record allows tracker screening only), literature figure panels, linac CBCT projections (MetaImage, not a C-arm), sets whose records exclude them, sets of fewer than 20 frames, true video files (no decoder without a new dependency) and archives never unpacked.

## How

- The segmentation model that labels the CTs is a block, `segmenter`, with three values: the one FleXray used (moosez, MOOSE 3), taken for every group that replicates FleXray's dataset; the project's own (SPINEPS), the default, taken for the production group; and `none`, for a recipe that reads no label, which only a render that aims at the volume or the crop takes. Only four render groups read masks: three on MOOSE's (the replica, the reference and the physical control) and one on SPINEPS's (the production group). Each value reads its own label alphabet and rewrites it to one shared id list, so an L6 is carried and said to be outside FleXray's protocol, and a T13 is counted and left out with its raw value recorded, rather than either being silently renumbered; no model is run inside a render, and SPINEPS is never run at all, since only existing masks are reused. Where a CT has no MOOSE labels, a job of its own makes them with moosez from fetched weights before the study renders. The study refuses nothing for a mask folder that is missing: a render group whose segmenter's masks are not there, or that fewer than five CTs have (the persons a group needs to be tested), is skipped with that reason while the others render, and its summary says how many CTs have each segmenter's masks. Every render group on one CT folder renders the same chosen CTs, chosen among those with the masks still required, and the analysis reads only the CTs every group kept, so each pair of render groups stays a paired sample of the same CTs.
- Every image takes one path: decoded at its own depth (DICOM through its modality LUT, MONOCHROME1 turned bone-bright), windowed to its 0.5 and 99.5 percentiles (the clip FleXray's paper applied to every image, X-ray or DRR), the centre square of 0.9 of its shorter side resized to 256. A cut square rather than a padded one keeps a pad's border from telling the corpora apart.
- Three feature spaces, so no finding rests on one encoder: FleXray's own encoder (the spatial mean of the input to the last instance norm of its deepest block, since the norm's output has the same spatial mean for every image), an ImageNet ResNet-18, and handcrafted physics measures (intensity histogram, power spectrum shape, gradients, noise, local contrast, clipping). The primary reading is inversion-invariant, the mean of an image's features and its inverse's, since fluoroscopy is often bone-dark and DRRs bone-bright.
- Maps: PCA, t-SNE (perplexity 30 on PCA-50), UMAP (30 neighbours on PCA-50) and LDA (supervised, labelled so), each but LDA with its trustworthiness.
- Tests: person-grouped classifier two-sample tests (logistic and 15 nearest neighbours) with person-cluster bootstrap intervals and person-level permutation p-values; the unbiased MMD with person-level permutations; Frechet distance and KID in each space's first 64 principal components; precision, recall, density and coverage; silhouette and neighbour purity; Benjamini-Hochberg within each space.
- Calibration: every corpus of 40 persons or more split in two by person and tested against itself, where every test must find nothing; real corpus against real corpus as the baseline a synthetic set is measured against, so that "separable" means farther than two real corpora are from each other, not merely distinguishable.
- Primary endpoints, fixed before any number: in FleXray's space, the C2ST AUC of FleXray's DRRs (and FluXray's, and the render from the CT alone) against pooled fluoroscopy and pooled film, and their Frechet distance against each family over the median distance between that family's own real corpora.

## What leaves the grid

Only printable text comes back from the grid: a summary of at most 80 lines of numbers and group names, carried by the bench report through the gate's redaction. The figures and the map coordinates go to the project's private W&B where the grid's key is found; the full results and the plan, which names files, stay on the grid.
